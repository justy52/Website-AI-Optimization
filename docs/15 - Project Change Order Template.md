Website and AI Optimization For Small Businesses

AI-FIRST OPERATING PRINCIPLE -> AUTHORITATIVE UPDATE

The business is intentionally designed around AI-agent-assisted fulfillment. AI agents perform the majority of repeatable collection, monitoring, analysis, drafting, prioritization, documentation, reporting, and safe internal execution. Human time is reserved for strategy, client relationships, judgment, quality control, and approval of consequential actions.

Agent permission levels:
1. OBSERVE -> read, crawl, monitor, measure, compare, and report. May run automatically.
2. PREPARE -> draft content, recommendations, schema, metadata, code changes, reports, tasks, and client communications. Output remains a draft until the applicable approval policy is satisfied.
3. EXECUTE -> perform only explicitly allowlisted, low-risk, reversible actions. Live-site publishing, destructive changes, contractual commitments, pricing changes, client-facing sends, and other consequential actions require human approval until a later policy explicitly permits otherwise.

Every material agent action must be tenant-scoped, logged, attributable to an agent/run, evidence-backed where factual claims are made, bounded by cost/time limits, and recoverable or safely retryable.


AI SCOPE RULE

AI efficiency does not make new deliverables "included." A request remains out of scope when it adds a new deliverable, location, page volume, integration, redesign, campaign, data source, or material execution responsibility beyond the contracted package. Agent-generated opportunities are recommendations until they fit within the current plan or an approved change order.

Use this template whenever a client requests work that changes the approved project scope, deliverables, timeline, cost, assumptions, or responsibilities. No out-of-scope work should begin until the change is documented and approved.

INTERNAL CHANGE-ORDER WORKFLOW

1. Receive Request  
Record the client's request in writing. If the request was made verbally, send a written summary and confirm it before pricing.

2. Check Scope  
Compare the request against the signed/approved proposal. Determine whether the request is:  
- Included in scope  
- A clarification of existing scope  
- A revision within the included allowance  
- Clearly out of scope  
- Caused by an unexpected technical condition

3. Estimate Impact  
Determine:  
- Additional work required  
- Additional cost  
- Timeline impact  
- New access or client materials required  
- Third-party costs  
- Risk to already completed work

4. Issue Change Order  
Send the written change order before beginning out-of-scope work.

5. Receive Approval  
Accept approval through the same contract/approval process used by the business. Preserve evidence of approval in the client project record.

6. Collect Required Payment  
If the change requires payment before work starts, do not begin until payment is received.

7. Update Project Records  
Update:  
- Scope checklist  
- Project total  
- Invoice/payment schedule  
- Timeline  
- Deliverables  
- QA requirements  
- Client action items

8. Complete & Document  
Perform the approved work and include it in final project documentation.

WHEN A FORMAL CHANGE ORDER MAY NOT BE NEEDED  
A formal change order may be unnecessary for a very small request that clearly falls within an existing included revision or maintenance allowance. Still document the request internally so scope usage is tracked.

WHEN A CHANGE ORDER IS REQUIRED  
Use one when the request:  
- Adds a page, location, integration, feature, automation, or major content deliverable  
- Changes approved direction after included revisions are exhausted  
- Requires substantial rework  
- Adds third-party software or recurring costs  
- Expands the website/platform covered  
- Requires custom development not originally scoped  
- Materially extends the timeline  
- Changes price or payment terms  
- Alters responsibilities or assumptions

SCOPE-PROTECTION RULE  
Never quietly absorb repeated or material out-of-scope requests just to avoid an uncomfortable conversation. Clear change orders protect both the client and the business by making expectations, costs, and timing explicit before work is performed.
