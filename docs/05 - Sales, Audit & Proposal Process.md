SALES, AUDIT & PROPOSAL PROCESS  
Website and AI Optimization For Small Businesses

AI-FIRST OPERATING PRINCIPLE -> AUTHORITATIVE UPDATE

The business is intentionally designed around AI-agent-assisted fulfillment. AI agents perform the majority of repeatable collection, monitoring, analysis, drafting, prioritization, documentation, reporting, and safe internal execution. Human time is reserved for strategy, client relationships, judgment, quality control, and approval of consequential actions.

Agent permission levels:
1. OBSERVE -> read, crawl, monitor, measure, compare, and report. May run automatically.
2. PREPARE -> draft content, recommendations, schema, metadata, code changes, reports, tasks, and client communications. Output remains a draft until the applicable approval policy is satisfied.
3. EXECUTE -> perform only explicitly allowlisted, low-risk, reversible actions. Live-site publishing, destructive changes, contractual commitments, pricing changes, client-facing sends, and other consequential actions require human approval until a later policy explicitly permits otherwise.

Every material agent action must be tenant-scoped, logged, attributable to an agent/run, evidence-backed where factual claims are made, bounded by cost/time limits, and recoverable or safely retryable.

PURPOSE  
This document defines the repeatable sales process from first contact through paid audit, proposal, acceptance, and project kickoff. The objective is to keep sales consultative, avoid giving away too much free work, and make the client understand the business value of the recommended improvements.

STANDARD SALES FLOW  
Prospect -> Qualification -> Mini Review -> Conversation -> Paid Audit -> Findings Review -> Proposal -> Follow-Up -> Acceptance -> Deposit -> Onboarding

1. PROSPECTING  
Good prospects are small businesses with a real website and visible opportunities to improve performance, conversion, search visibility, local visibility, trust, or AI discoverability.

Potential lead sources:  
- Existing business relationships  
- Referrals  
- Local networking  
- Google Maps / Google Business Profile searches  
- Direct outreach  
- Social media  
- Community groups  
- Industry associations  
- Inbound website leads

2. QUICK QUALIFICATION  
Before doing unpaid analysis, answer:  
- Is this an active legitimate business?  
- Is there a functioning website to evaluate?  
- Is the site materially important to lead generation or credibility?  
- Are there obvious improvement opportunities?  
- Can the owner or decision-maker be reached?  
- Is the likely work within the services offered?

If the answer is mostly no, do not spend significant time on the lead.

3. FREE MINI REVIEW  
The free review is intentionally limited. Its purpose is to create a useful conversation, not replace the paid audit.

Identify approximately 3-5 clear observations, for example:  
- Important page is very slow on mobile  
- Call-to-action is weak or hard to find  
- Service information is unclear  
- Contact form has friction  
- Key local-service pages are missing  
- Page titles/headings are poorly structured  
- Website does not clearly explain location/service area  
- Trust signals are weak  
- Content is difficult for search/AI systems to interpret

Do not prepare a comprehensive remediation plan for free.

4. FIRST CONTACT  
The tone should be helpful and specific, not alarmist.

Example positioning:  
"I was looking at your website and noticed a few things that may be making it harder for potential customers to find you or take the next step. I do website and AI optimization for small businesses, and I can show you a few of the issues I noticed."

The goal is to earn a conversation, not close the entire project in the first message.

5. DISCOVERY CONVERSATION  
Learn what the owner actually needs.

Questions to cover:  
- What are the most important services you want more business for?  
- Where do most new customers come from now?  
- What role does the website play in the sales process?  
- What is the most important action a visitor should take?  
- Are there services or locations you want to grow?  
- Do you know who manages the website now?  
- Are you currently paying an SEO, marketing, or web company?  
- Do you have Google Analytics/Search Console access?  
- What frustrates you about the current site?  
- If the site worked better, what would success look like?

6. SELL THE PAID AUDIT  
Default Price: $495

Position the audit as the next step when there is enough potential value to justify deeper analysis.

Explain that the audit will prioritize the site rather than produce a generic automated score.

The client should understand they receive:  
- Performance/usability findings  
- Conversion findings  
- SEO/local visibility findings  
- AI-readiness/content-structure findings  
- Tracking/measurement findings where available  
- Prioritized recommendations  
- Clear explanation of what should be fixed first

Payment should be collected before the audit begins.

7. AUDIT EXECUTION  
Create a baseline before recommendations.

Review relevant areas such as:  
- Mobile experience  
- Page speed/Core Web Vitals  
- Navigation  
- Calls to action  
- Forms and lead paths  
- Technical SEO basics  
- Search indexing issues  
- Titles, metadata, headings  
- Service and location content  
- Internal linking  
- Google Business Profile alignment  
- Trust/reputation signals  
- Analytics/tracking  
- Structured data/schema  
- AI discoverability/readability  
- FAQ and customer-question coverage

PRIORITY SYSTEM  
Critical -> broken/risky/directly losing opportunities  
High -> meaningful impact and worth addressing promptly  
Medium -> valuable but secondary  
Low -> polish/future opportunity

For each important finding explain:  
Problem -> Why it matters -> Recommended action

8. FINDINGS REVIEW  
Present the biggest issues first. Do not overwhelm the owner with technical terminology.

Translate technical findings into business language.

Instead of:  
"Your LCP is 5.2 seconds."

Say:  
"The main content is taking too long to appear on mobile, which can cause visitors to leave before they ever see your offer."

Show enough evidence that the client understands the recommendation.

9. TRANSITION FROM AUDIT TO PROPOSAL  
At the end of the audit review, summarize what should happen next.

Example:  
"The highest-value next step is to fix these first eight items. I can package those into one Optimization Launch rather than having you coordinate multiple vendors."

Do not pressure the client into purchasing every recommendation.

10. PROPOSAL STRUCTURE  
Every proposal should include:  
- Client/business  
- Website  
- Summary of current situation  
- Project objective  
- Specific deliverables  
- Explicit exclusions  
- Client responsibilities  
- Required access  
- Timeline or delivery sequence  
- Price  
- Payment terms  
- Revision/change process  
- Assumptions  
- Acceptance instructions

For a straightforward site, the Optimization Launch may start at $1,500. Larger or more complex projects should be priced based on actual scope.

11. SCOPE CONTROL  
Avoid vague language such as:  
- "Complete SEO"  
- "Fix everything"  
- "Unlimited website changes"  
- "Get you to \#1 on Google"  
- "Make AI recommend your business"

Use specific deliverables instead.

Example:  
"Rewrite and restructure the home page and three primary service pages, improve headings and calls to action, implement agreed technical SEO fixes, and add FAQ/structured content identified in the audit."

12. FOLLOW-UP PROCESS  
A proposal should never disappear without a next action.

Suggested follow-up cadence:  
- Proposal delivery: explain the recommendation and answer questions  
- First follow-up: confirm receipt and ask whether anything needs clarification  
- Second follow-up: address decision barriers and confirm whether the project is still a priority  
- Final follow-up: politely close the loop and leave the door open

Avoid daily chasing or manipulative urgency.

13. OBJECTION HANDLING  
"IT COSTS TOO MUCH"  
Clarify which business problem the scope addresses. If needed, reduce scope or phase the project rather than discounting the same amount of work.

"I NEED TO THINK ABOUT IT"  
Ask what specifically they still need to evaluate: timing, cost, trust, scope, another decision-maker, or competing priorities.

"MY WEB GUY CAN DO IT"  
That may be appropriate. The audit can still provide the prioritized roadmap. Do not attack another provider.

"CAN YOU GUARANTEE RANKINGS/LEADS?"  
No. Explain that search engines, AI systems, competitors, the client's offer, market conditions, and other factors are outside direct control. Commit to the defined optimization work and transparent measurement instead.

14. ACCEPTANCE  
A project is accepted only when the required commercial conditions are complete:  
- Written approval/agreement  
- Deposit or required initial payment received  
- Client understands scope  
- Required onboarding/access process initiated

Do not begin unpaid production work because a client verbally says, "Go ahead."

15. HANDOFF TO DELIVERY  
Create the client project folder and move/copy the approved materials into it:  
- Lead/discovery notes  
- Paid audit  
- Proposal/agreement  
- Payment record  
- Access checklist  
- Baseline data  
- Implementation plan

Then change the project stage to Onboarding / Awaiting Access or Active Production.

CORE SALES RULES  
- Give useful insight, not a free full audit.  
- Diagnose before prescribing.  
- Speak in business outcomes.  
- Sell a defined scope.  
- Do not compete purely on price.  
- Reduce scope before discounting labor.  
- Never promise rankings, AI placement, leads, or revenue.  
- Make the next step clear after every client interaction.
