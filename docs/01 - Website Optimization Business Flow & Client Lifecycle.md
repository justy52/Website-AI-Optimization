WEBSITE OPTIMIZATION BUSINESS FLOW & CLIENT LIFECYCLE

PURPOSE  

AI-FIRST OPERATING PRINCIPLE -> AUTHORITATIVE UPDATE

The business is intentionally designed around AI-agent-assisted fulfillment. AI agents perform the majority of repeatable collection, monitoring, analysis, drafting, prioritization, documentation, reporting, and safe internal execution. Human time is reserved for strategy, client relationships, judgment, quality control, and approval of consequential actions.

Agent permission levels:
1. OBSERVE -> read, crawl, monitor, measure, compare, and report. May run automatically.
2. PREPARE -> draft content, recommendations, schema, metadata, code changes, reports, tasks, and client communications. Output remains a draft until the applicable approval policy is satisfied.
3. EXECUTE -> perform only explicitly allowlisted, low-risk, reversible actions. Live-site publishing, destructive changes, contractual commitments, pricing changes, client-facing sends, and other consequential actions require human approval until a later policy explicitly permits otherwise.

Every material agent action must be tenant-scoped, logged, attributable to an agent/run, evidence-backed where factual claims are made, bounded by cost/time limits, and recoverable or safely retryable.


UPDATED DEFAULT CLIENT PATH

Prospect -> Digital Visibility Audit ($495) -> Optimization Launch ($1,500+) -> Monthly Optimization (Essentials $750 / Growth $1,250 / Pro $2,000).

The app should automate as much of this lifecycle as practical: lead enrichment, pre-sales scan, audit evidence collection, finding drafts, proposal inputs, onboarding tasks, recurring monitoring, monthly opportunity generation, content/optimization drafts, verification, and reporting. Human review is concentrated at the points that affect client commitments or live production systems.

This document defines how the business should move a small-business client from first contact through completed optimization work and, when appropriate, into ongoing support. The goal is a simple, repeatable system that can be handled efficiently by a small operation without creating unnecessary overhead.

CORE BUSINESS MODEL  
The business sells practical website improvement services to small businesses that already have a website but are losing opportunities because the site is slow, confusing, outdated, hard to use on mobile, weak at converting visitors, or poorly structured for search engines and AI-assisted discovery.

The operating principle is: diagnose first, prioritize what matters, make measurable improvements, document the work, and give the client a clear path forward.

CLIENT LIFECYCLE

1. Lead Generation  
Potential clients can come from direct outreach, referrals, local networking, Google Business Profile searches, social media, existing business relationships, or inbound website inquiries.

Every lead should enter one simple tracking system with:  
- Business name  
- Contact name  
- Website URL  
- Phone/email  
- Lead source  
- Initial problem noticed  
- Current stage  
- Next action/date

2. Quick Qualification  
Before spending significant time on a prospect, confirm:  
- The business is legitimate and actively operating.  
- It has an existing website or a clear need for optimization work.  
- There are visible opportunities to improve the website.  
- The decision-maker can be identified.  
- The likely project is within the services the business is prepared to deliver.

Avoid giving away a full audit during qualification. The purpose is to determine whether the prospect is worth pursuing.

3. Initial Website Review  
Perform a short pre-sales review focused on obvious, high-impact problems such as:  
- Mobile usability  
- Page speed and Core Web Vitals  
- Broken links or broken layouts  
- Calls to action  
- Contact forms  
- Navigation  
- Trust signals  
- Local SEO basics  
- Page titles and descriptions  
- Content clarity  
- Conversion friction  
- Accessibility red flags  
- Analytics/tracking gaps

Use this review to identify 3-5 concrete opportunities that can be discussed with the prospect.

4. Discovery Conversation  
The discovery conversation should establish:  
- What the business sells  
- Who its ideal customer is  
- What actions the website should generate  
- Current marketing channels  
- Known website frustrations  
- Who controls the domain, hosting, CMS, analytics, and Google Business Profile  
- Whether another agency or developer is involved  
- Urgency and budget expectations  
- What success would look like

The goal is to understand the business problem behind the website problem.

5. Audit / Assessment  
For qualified clients, perform a structured audit. Separate findings into:  
- Critical: broken, risky, or directly costing leads  
- High priority: meaningful performance/conversion/search problems  
- Medium priority: worthwhile improvements  
- Low priority: polish or future opportunities

Each finding should explain:  
- What is wrong  
- Why it matters  
- Recommended fix  
- Estimated effort or project phase

6. Proposal  
The proposal should convert the audit into a defined scope rather than promising to "improve the website" generally.

Every proposal should state:  
- Current problems being addressed  
- Specific deliverables  
- What is excluded  
- Client responsibilities  
- Required access  
- Timeline or delivery sequence  
- Price and payment terms  
- Revision/change-request process  
- Ownership and handoff terms

Whenever possible, package work by outcome or project scope rather than selling undifferentiated hourly labor.

7. Agreement & Payment  
Do not begin production work until the client has accepted the scope and completed the required initial payment.

Create a project record containing:  
- Signed agreement  
- Invoice/payment status  
- Client contacts  
- Website and account access  
- Original audit  
- Approved scope  
- Baseline metrics/screenshots  
- Backup/rollback information

8. Client Intake & Access  
Collect only the access needed for the approved work. Common systems may include:  
- Website CMS  
- Hosting provider  
- Domain/DNS  
- Google Analytics  
- Google Search Console  
- Google Business Profile  
- Tag Manager  
- Email/form provider  
- CDN/security provider  
- Existing SEO or marketing tools

Use client-created user accounts or delegated access whenever possible rather than asking clients to share master passwords.

9. Baseline & Backup  
Before changing the site:  
- Record current performance metrics.  
- Capture key page screenshots.  
- Confirm analytics are working.  
- Create or confirm a current backup.  
- Identify the rollback process.  
- Confirm whether a staging environment exists.

No substantial production change should be made without knowing how to reverse it.

10. Optimization Work  
Execute work in priority order:  
A. Stability/security/broken functionality  
B. Mobile and usability issues  
C. Speed/performance  
D. Conversion and calls to action  
E. Technical SEO  
F. On-page SEO/content structure  
G. Analytics and measurement  
H. Lower-priority visual polish

Work should be documented as it is completed so the final report does not have to be reconstructed from memory.

11. Quality Assurance  
Before client handoff, verify:  
- Desktop and mobile layouts  
- Primary navigation  
- Forms and calls to action  
- Phone/email links  
- Major browsers where practical  
- Page speed/performance changes  
- Analytics and tracking  
- Search/indexing settings  
- No accidental noindex, broken redirects, or missing metadata  
- Backup/rollback still available

12. Client Review  
Show the client:  
- What changed  
- Why it changed  
- Before/after examples  
- Any measurable improvement  
- Anything still recommended but outside the current scope

Collect one consolidated round of feedback whenever possible rather than allowing scattered requests across text, email, and phone calls.

13. Closeout  
At project completion:  
- Resolve approved final revisions.  
- Confirm the site is functioning.  
- Deliver the final report.  
- Return or remove unnecessary access.  
- Confirm final payment.  
- Document future recommendations.  
- Ask for a review/referral when appropriate.

14. Ongoing Support / Retainer  
Not every client needs a retainer. Offer ongoing service when there is a recurring business need, such as:  
- Monthly performance monitoring  
- Content updates  
- SEO improvements  
- Conversion testing  
- Analytics/reporting  
- Technical maintenance  
- Quarterly optimization reviews

Retainers should have a defined recurring scope, response expectation, and limits.

STANDARD PROJECT STAGES  
Lead -> Qualified -> Discovery -> Audit -> Proposal -> Accepted -> Intake -> Baseline/Backup -> Optimization -> QA -> Client Review -> Closeout -> Ongoing Support or Completed

OPERATING RULES  
- No major work without a defined scope.  
- No production changes without backup/rollback awareness.  
- Avoid storing client master passwords when delegated access is available.  
- Document every meaningful change.  
- Tie recommendations to business outcomes, not technical jargon.  
- Keep communication centralized.  
- Do not promise rankings, revenue, or results that cannot be guaranteed.  
- Protect scope: new requests become approved change orders or future work.

SUCCESS MEASURES  
The business should track:  
- Leads generated  
- Qualified leads  
- Discovery calls  
- Proposals sent  
- Proposal close rate  
- Average project value  
- Time from acceptance to completion  
- Gross margin/time spent per project  
- Client satisfaction/reviews  
- Referrals  
- Retainer conversion rate  
- Measurable website improvements where applicable
